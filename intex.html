About Us

United Against Drugs NYC was born as an urgent response to the addiction crisis affecting the Bronx. Our founder, David Daza, witnessed this reality both in Colombia and in New York and understood that addiction is not fought from a distance, but by being present where it is most needed. That is why our organization combines community prevention, family support, recreational activities, and direct outreach on the streets to offer real help to those facing addiction. We believe in dignity, empathy, and action: we don't wait for people to come to us, we go to them, because no one should face this problem alone.

Mission Statement

Our mission is to confront the addiction crisis in the Bronx with direct action, empathy, and a constant presence. At United Against Drugs NYC, we work to prevent substance use among youth, support affected families, and offer immediate help to those struggling with addiction on the streets. We believe in human dignity, the strength of community, and that every person deserves a real chance at recovery. That's why we don't wait for people to come to us: we go to them, bringing food, guidance, recreational activities, and emotional support where it's needed most.

Our Services

In this section, you'll find the solutions we offer directly to the community. Each service is designed to support, guide, and respond to the real needs of people living on the streets of the Bronx.

Solution 1:

  • Our organization will dispatch a team to canvass numerous streets in high-risk areas of the city, distributing flyers and alerting the community to upcoming park events in the vicinity. These events will feature food and activities for children—incentives designed to encourage greater attendance among low-income residents. We begin with young children to raise awareness regarding the harmful and dangerous nature of drugs; we provide educational guides and display impactful imagery to ensure a lasting impression, thereby making the children fully aware of the terrible damage caused by substances such as drugs.

Solution 2:

  • Instead of waiting for people to come to us, we will rely on a dedicated team to hit the streets in search of those who are truly in eed of help. We know from experience that many individuals battling addiction also suffer from hunger; therefore, as an incentive, we will provide them with nutritious food and basic necessities—such as a haircut, a change of clothes, or a hearty meal shared in close fellowship—as this creates a greater opportunity for them to place their trust in us. Once we have established a solid bond with these individuals, our mental health support team and social workers will step in to converse with them and listen to the stories they need to share. All of this will take place right on the streets where we find them, recognizing that conducting these interactions in a formal setting might make them feel uncomfortable or as though they are being pressured. Many of them simply need someone to listen to them and to feel that they matter to others; this, too, will help them feel at ease and understand that we are here to help.

Solution 3:

  • Another significant reason why people turn to drugs is family problems. We offer recreational programs—such as sports—to provide our youth with a positive outlet and introduce them to new activities they might enjoy, helping them take their minds off the other difficulties they face. Many young people suffer from family issues, which is why they develop drug addictions; in such cases, we begin by working with the families, meeting with them prior to the individual's initial appointment. Our goal is to help families understand that recovery is a process and to assure them that we will be there to support them. Once the family is ready, we initiate the detoxification process, which includes family therapy sessions. Subsequently, we assist the individual in finding employment—as well as academic support for those who need it—while also providing daily nutritional assistance.
